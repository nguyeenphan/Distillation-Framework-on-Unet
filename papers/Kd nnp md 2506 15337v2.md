# Knowledge Distillation Framework for Accelerating High-Accuracy Neural Network-Based Molecular Dynamics Simulations

**Authors:** Naoki Matsumura, Yuta Yoshimoto, Yuto Iwasaki, Meguru Yamazaki, and Yasufumi Sakai

Fujitsu Research, Fujitsu Limited, 4-1-1, Kamiodanaka, Nakahara-ku, Kawasaki, Kanagawa 211-8588, Japan

**arXiv:** 2506.15337v2 [cs.LG] 20 Jun 2025

## Abstract

Neural network potentials (NNPs) offer a powerful alternative to traditional force fields for molecular dynamics (MD) simulations. Accurate and stable MD simulations, crucial for evaluating material properties, require training data encompassing both low-energy stable structures and high-energy structures. Conventional knowledge distillation (KD) methods fine-tune a pre-trained NNP as a teacher model to generate training data for a student model. However, in material-specific models, this fine-tuning process increases energy barriers, making it difficult to create training data containing high-energy structures. To address this, we propose a novel KD framework that leverages a non-fine-tuned, off-the-shelf pre-trained NNP as a teacher. Its gentler energy landscape facilitates the exploration of a wider range of structures, including the high-energy structures crucial for stable MD simulations. Our framework employs a two-stage training process: first, the student NNP is trained with a dataset generated by the off-the-shelf teacher; then, it is fine-tuned with a smaller, high-accuracy density functional theory (DFT) dataset. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our framework by applying it to both organic (polyethylene glycol) and inorganic (L₁₀GeP₂S₁₂) materials, achieving comparable or superior accuracy in reproducing physical properties compared to existing methods. Importantly, our method reduces the number of expensive DFT calculations by 10x compared to existing NNP generation methods, without sacrificing accuracy. Furthermore, the resulting student NNP achieves up to 10⁶x speedup in inference compared to the teacher NNP, enabling significantly faster and more efficient MD simulations.

**Keywords:** Knowledge Distillation · Neural Network Potential · Molecular Dynamics

## 1. Introduction

Neural network potentials (NNPs) represent a significant advancement in interatomic potential modeling, offering a data-driven alternative to traditional empirical force fields. By leveraging the representational power of neural networks, NNPs learn the complex potential energy surface (PES) directly from high-accuracy ab initio calculations, such as density functional theory (DFT). This approach overcomes the limitations of fixed functional forms inherent in empirical potentials, enabling accurate molecular dynamics (MD) simulations and rapid materials screening.

To further enhance the prediction accuracy of NNPs, researchers have increasingly focused on developing larger and more complex models. As a result, the learning of large datasets has become possible, leading to the development of universal NNPs applicable to a wide range of materials (M3GNet, CHGNet, MatterSim). While these efforts have led to improved accuracy and versatility, they have also resulted in a significant increase in computational cost. This slowdown in inference speed limits the applicability of these large NNPs in computationally demanding applications, such as large-scale, long-time MD simulations.

To address this limitation, knowledge distillation (KD) has emerged as a promising technique. KD transfers knowledge from a larger, accurate "teacher" model to a smaller "student" model, enabling faster inference speeds. Several KD methods have been proposed for NNPs in MD simulations. These methods typically begin by fine-tuning a pre-trained universal NNP with DFT data to create a teacher model that accurately reproduces the DFT PES. However, fine-tuning the teacher model also increases the energy barriers in MD simulations, making it difficult to explore structures in high-energy regions (crucial for improving the robustness of the NNP).

Here, we present a novel KD framework for generating high-accuracy and computationally efficient NNPs to realize stable MD simulation. To effectively explore structures in high-energy regions, we leverage a non-fine-tuned, off-the-shelf pre-trained NNP as a teacher. Its gentler energy landscape facilitates the exploration of a wider range of structures.

### Contributions

- We propose a novel knowledge distillation framework for generating high-accuracy and computationally efficient NNPs to realize long-time MD simulation of large-scale material systems.
- Our key proposal is training a student model with soft targets generated by a non-fine-tuned, off-the-shelf teacher model, enabling the generation of diverse structures in high-energy regions and more robust MD simulation.
- We demonstrate that our proposed framework achieves comparable or superior accuracy in reproducing experimental properties of PEG and LGPS compared to existing methods.
- Our framework reduces the number of hard targets generated using expensive DFT calculations by 10x compared to existing NNP generation methods, without sacrificing accuracy.

## 2. Related Work

### Molecular Simulations with NNPs

An NNP model typically consists of two key neural networks: a descriptor and fitting networks. The descriptor network transforms the atomic environment of each atom into a set of numerical features that are invariant to translation, rotation, and permutation of atoms. These features are then input to the fitting network, which maps the features to the potential energy and atomic forces.

### Generating NNPs: From Ab Initio MD to Universal Models

NNPs are typically trained on datasets of atomic structures with corresponding energies and forces. Active learning (AL) methods have emerged as a promising alternative for efficient data acquisition. Including configurations within high-energy regions is crucial for improving NNP robustness. More recently, universal NNP models trained on vast datasets (M3GNet, CHGNet, MatterSim) have emerged, but their slow inference speeds and large memory footprints limit their use in large-scale simulations.

### Knowledge Distillation

Knowledge distillation (KD) is a technique for model compression and acceleration. The main objective of KD is transferring knowledge from a large and complex teacher model to a lightweight and fast student model. Within NNPs, existing methods typically fine-tune a pre-trained universal NNP with DFT data to construct a teacher model. However, this adjustment elevates energy barriers in MD simulations, hindering exploration of structures in high-energy regions. Therefore, we use a non-fine-tuned, off-the-shelf teacher model with a gentler PES shape to generate soft targets.

## 3. Knowledge Distillation Framework

The framework consists of four stages:

### (a) Soft Target Generation

NNP-MD simulations are performed using the off-the-shelf teacher model to generate a comprehensive set of atomic structures. The NNP-MD simulation parameters (temperature, pressure, simulation time) are carefully chosen to cover conditions relevant to the downstream task. Generated structures are labeled with soft targets (energy and force) reflecting the teacher model's knowledge of the PES.

### (b) Learning with Soft Targets

A student model is trained using the soft targets. This stage aims to train the student model on the PES of the teacher model and to enrich the descriptors of the student model. Mean squared error (MSE) loss is employed. The resulting trained student model functions as a structural selector for generating hard targets and as a pre-trained model for fine-tuning.

### (c) Hard Target Generation

To further improve accuracy, a representative subset of structures is selected from the soft targets and converted into hard targets using DFT calculations. The structural feature-based screening method (GeNNIP4MD) is used for efficient data point selection: intermediate layer features are extracted from the student model, compressed into 2D feature space using densMAP, augmented with a normalized energy dimension to create 3D space, and data points are selected by maximizing inter-point distances.

### (d) Fine-tuning with Hard Targets

The student model is fine-tuned using the hard target dataset. Since the underlying atomic structures are essentially identical to soft targets (differing only in labels), learned descriptors are fixed and only the fitting network is optimized.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Accuracy Verification for PEG (Polyethylene Glycol)

**Setup:**
- Five initial structures at densities of 1.120 g/cm³ ± 5%
- Teacher model: MatterSim-v1.0.0-5M
- NNP-MD simulations: 150 ps, 0.5 fs timestep, isothermal-isochoric ensemble at 300–600 K
- 16,000 training / 4,000 validation soft targets
- Student model: DeepPot-SE (DP) with se_e2_a descriptor, cutoff radius 6Å
- 1,000 hard targets selected via structural feature-based screening

**Force MAE Results:**
- Student (soft targets only): 0.204 eV/Å (teacher: 0.202 eV/Å)
- Fine-tuned student: 0.061 eV/Å

**Comparison with Existing KD Methods:**

Existing KD methods fine-tune the teacher model first, then generate soft targets. A student model trained on these "Soft targets (Fine-tuned)" exhibited a force MAE of 0.063 eV/Å but produced a density of only 0.015 g/cm³ in MD simulations — a complete failure. Energy histograms show that the fine-tuned teacher's soft targets exhibit an energy distribution shifted to lower values, indicating insufficient sampling in the high-energy region. This confirms the advantage of using a non-fine-tuned teacher model.

### 4.2 Accuracy Verification for LGPS (L₁₀GeP₂S₁₂)

**Setup:**
- Teacher: MatterSim-v1.0.0-5M
- NNP-MD simulations: 110 ps, 0.5 fs timestep, 300–1500 K (300 K intervals)
- 8,000 training / 2,000 validation soft targets

**Force MAE Results:**
- Student (soft targets only): 0.088 eV/Å (teacher: 0.085 eV/Å)
- Fine-tuned student: 0.054 eV/Å

The fine-tuned student model demonstrates excellent agreement with AIMD and experimental lithium-ion self-diffusion coefficients across a wide temperature range, using only 1/5 the data points required by GeNNIP4MD.

### 4.3 Evaluation of Computational Performance

**Inference Speedup (Student vs. Teacher):**
- PEG: 10x to 10⁶x speedup (82x for the 3,100-atom production system)
- LGPS: 5x to 46x speedup (20x for the 1,600-atom production system)

The teacher model's high memory usage restricts PEG simulations to a maximum of 4,096 atoms, while the student model enables simulations of up to 19,840 atoms.

## 5. Conclusion

We have presented a novel KD framework for generating lightweight and high-accuracy NNPs tailored for MD simulations. Unlike existing KD methods that fine-tune a teacher model and subsequently sample low-energy configurations, our approach leverages a pre-trained universal NNP to explore high-energy regions, increasing the probability of including representative structures for improving the robustness of NNPs in the training dataset. The student model is initially trained on this dataset, and then refined through fine-tuning with high-accuracy DFT calculations on strategically selected data points. Results on PEG and LGPS demonstrate that our framework can generate NNPs that accurately reproduce physical properties while reducing the computational cost of NNP-MD simulations by up to two orders of magnitude compared to the teacher model. This framework offers a promising approach for accelerating materials simulations by enabling the efficient generation of accurate and computationally affordable NNPs.
